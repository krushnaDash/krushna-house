## 3. The options

**C – plan A re-planned (pages 1–2)**
- Kitchen moves to the west end (8′-6″ × 12′-0″, 102 sq ft, was 66). Bath 2 sits between the kitchen and the living/dining, shares a wet wall with the kitchen and opens off the lobby.
- Same split as A on the C line: 2 BHK west, 1 BHK east. Connecting door 3′-0″ in the C wall, locked on page 1, open on page 2.
- 1 BHK bedroom door moved to the west end of its partition, so the TV unit no longer stands in front of it.

**D – 1 BHK west, 2 BHK next to the stair (pages 3–4)**
- Party wall moves to the B line. 1 BHK = bedroom + bath + 6′ galley kitchen + living at its own entrance. 2 BHK = master with attached bath, living, dining/kitchen, Bedroom 2, Bath 2 and the balcony.
- No passage: every door opens straight into a living area. A wide opening between C2 and C3 joins the 2 BHK living and dining.
- Cost: the 2 BHK drops from 698 to 614 sq ft. Option C keeps it at 700.

**E1 – full 3 BHK, balcony kept (page 5)**
- Only door on the front wall, beside the stair, into the living room. No second entrance, no connecting door.
- Living (16′ × 10′) → 9′-9″ wide opening → dining → lobby → kitchen / Bath 2 / master. Bedroom 2 opens off the dining. Bedroom 3 (NE, attached Bath 3, balcony) opens off the living.

**E2 – E1 with the back strip enclosed (page 6)**
- Master suite in the NE: bedroom 168 + bath 30 + dressing room 26 sq ft. The old master becomes Bedroom 3. No balcony.
- Biggest carpet area (1,083) but it closes a cantilever, see section 5.

**E3 – central kitchen–dining spine (page 7)**
- Kitchen (12′-7″ × 7′-7″, 95 sq ft, north window) across the back of the middle bay. L-shaped dining hall in the middle serves the kitchen, guest WC, master, Bedroom 2 and the living (through the 9′-9″ opening).
- Three en-suite bedrooms (west wing: master, Bedroom 2; east wing: Bedroom 3) plus a guest WC. No lobby or passage.
- Most plumbing of all options.

## 5. Assumptions to confirm ⚠

1. The stair lands at the east end (D3) of the front gallery, as in plan A. "Near" and "far" in D, and the single entrance position in E, follow from that.
2. The 9′-9″ openings in the C line (D, E1, E2, E3) need a beam over them. Ask the structural engineer.
3. E2 closes the 4′ back cantilever with a wall and glazing, which adds load at the slab tip. It also needs a bye-law check (FAR, setback) before you choose it.
4. The kitchen at the west end (C, E1, E2) takes the left 4′ strip, with a window on the highway wall. You allowed windows and baths there and suggested the kitchen at the end, so I assumed this is fine. A fixed or high-level window is an option if the highway noise or dust bothers you.
5. Slab/beam layout, floor height and the cantilever points are the same open items as in plan A, section 7.
6. Furniture is indicative, no compass direction is given, so no Vastu rules are applied.
